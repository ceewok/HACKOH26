HackOHI/O 2026

Bridge Builder Challenge

Design a platform where students learn together and connect with industry.

Background

Classes teach a lot, but rarely what it is like to work on a real team, ship on a deadline, or learn from people in

industry. At the same time, AI now writes many resumes, so a list of skills says less than ever about what

someone can actually do.

Imagine a LinkedIn, GitHub, and Kaggle built just for Ohio State students and industry: one place to connect,

build, and learn together. Students meet peers across majors, join short real-world projects of 4 to 6 weeks with

company teams, and learn from each other along the way. What they build, and how they help others, becomes a

record that AI cannot generate.

Challenge

Design and build one part of a community platform that connects students with each other and with

industry, so they learn by doing real work together.

Pick one part of the platform and build a working demo of it. Assume the program already exists, and

invent your own sample students, projects, and mentors.

Who It Is For

• Students: Want to learn, meet people, and build real experience, but have limited time.

• Industry mentors: Professionals who would gladly give an hour, but rarely six weeks.

• Ohio State: Faculty and advisors who want students learning beyond the classroom.

The Ladder

Communities grow when joining is easy and each step leads to the next. Think of the platform as a ladder:

1. Look around Browse projects, demos, and what other students learned.

2. Show up Ask a question, join a topic group, or drop into industry office hours.

3. Try something small Take on a one-day challenge or give feedback on a peer's demo.

4. Join a project Work 4 to 6 weeks on a team with a company mentor.

5. Give back Mentor newer students, lead a topic group, or help shape the next project.

Ideas to Explore

How might students find people to learn and build with?

How might students and industry meet and learn from each other?

How might people share their work and get useful feedback?

How might growth become visible in a way others can trust?

How might what one group learns help the next?

Pick one, combine a few, or find your own question.

Things to Consider

Solve the Empty Room

Why would the first 50 students join a space with nobody in it? Think about what makes people show up early and come back.

Respect Everyone's Time

Students are busy, and industry mentors are busier. The best features ask for minutes, not hours.

Community, Not a Job Board

If it feels like recruiting, people perform instead of learn. Keep the focus on helping, building, and growing together.

Make Growth Visible

The strongest record is earned from others: work shipped, help given, feedback received. Using AI in your tool is welcome when it

helps people connect and learn, not when it generates polish.

Think Big but Start Small

Your vision can be ambitious, but your demo can be simple. One complete flow beats five half-built features.

Judging

Solutions will be judged on:

• Problem Solving: How well your solution helps students connect, learn, and keep coming back.

• Creativity: The originality and innovation of your approach.

• Clarity: How clearly your team explains the problem and your solution in the video pitch.

• Completeness: How well your demo shows one complete flow, with all necessary components.

Kickstart Your Idea: A Simple Guide

1. Pick Your Part

• Choose one rung of the ladder, or one connection: student to student, or student to industry.

• Ask what makes that step hard today, and what would make you take it.

2. Design Your Solution

• Think about how it improves one or more of the following:

• Connection: How do people find and get to know each other?

• Learning: How do students build skills by working with others?

• Momentum: What brings people back and moves them up the ladder?

• Trust: How does real work become something others can rely on?

3. Build a Working Demo

• Build one flow end to end: a web or mobile app, an AI agent, or a clickable prototype. Sample data is fine.

4. Create a Video Pitch (DO NOT WAIT UNTIL THE LAST FEW MINUTES TO UPLOAD)

• Length: 3ish minutes. Include a link to your demo or code if you have one.

• Content:

• Introduce your team and project.

• Explain the part of the platform you chose and why it matters.

• Demo your solution and how it works.

• Share how it helps students connect and learn.

• Highlight what you learned during development.
